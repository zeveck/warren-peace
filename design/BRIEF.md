# Brief: Warren Peace

**The game in one sentence:** a young rabbit whose warren is wrecked by maddened neighbours goes down through a door that was never meant to open, gathers friends from the storybook worlds and the strange deep places beneath every warren, and builds an army that frees each warren in turn, until all of them stand together on the downs at peace.

**The feeling:** cozy-epic. Warm grass, low sun, friends who tease each other, a growing band of oddballs who'd follow you anywhere. Underneath, a quiet chill that gets colder with every layer down: carvings that look back, whispers in tunnels, wrong-angled rooms. Eerie, never gory, never hopeless. The war is sad rather than cruel; the enemy rabbits are victims to be woken, not villains to be killed. All ages.

## Who, where, how long

- Players of all ages who like cozy RPGs and storybooks; a parent and child could play it together.
- Desktop browser, keyboard and mouse (`--platform desktop`). No phone layout or touch controls.
- A long game: roughly 3 to 4 hours to finish, played in sessions of 20 to 45 minutes, with saving anywhere on the surface and autosaves at every door.

## Style references

No `--style` URLs or named games were given. The reference is a mood, plus the source books:

- **Watership Down (the mood only).** Borrow: chalk downland, the warren as home and community, rabbits as small, brave and vulnerable, wind and weather, a gentle melancholy. **Avoid copying:** its names (no Hazel, Fiver, Bigwig, Woundwort, Efrafa, Sandleford, El-ahrairah, the Black Rabbit), its invented rabbit language, its plot, and its violence.
- **The Wind in the Willows** (Grahame, 1908), **Alice's Adventures in Wonderland** (Carroll, 1865), **The Wonderful Wizard of Oz** (Baum, 1900), **Journey to the Center of the Earth** (Verne, 1864), and the folk tale of the **Moon Rabbit** (the jade rabbit pounding the elixir on the moon): all public domain. Use the books: in Oz the shoes are **silver**, there are Kalidahs, Hammer-Heads, the Queen of the Field Mice, the Golden Cap that commands the Winged Monkeys, and the China Country. Nothing from later films (no ruby slippers, no film costumes or songs).
- **The Lovecraft thread (the feel only).** Borrow: the ancient sleeper below, carvings in no known hand, whispering stone, geometry that's wrong, the fear of what's deeper. **Avoid:** Lovecraft's named creatures and places, and anything grisly. Our creature is our own.
- **Look:** isometric pixel art in warm downland colours above and cooler, stranger colours below; chunky readable sprites; cozy interiors in the warren.

## Settings and decisions made for the person

- Platform desktop; target web; scope large; budget $20; 2 review rounds; plan only; game in this folder.
- **Title: "Warren Peace"**, the person's own idea; it fits the story exactly (a war between warrens that ends in peace) and is warm.
- **The creature: the Undersleeper,** a vast, ancient dreamer beneath all the depths. It isn't evil: it's asleep, and digging too deep disturbs its sleep. Its bad dreams leak upward as **the Hum**, a low drone in the soil that gets into rabbits' heads, making them forget their names and homes and want only to dig deeper and take more. It's broken not by killing anything but by calming it: freed rabbits are "woken" in battle, and in the finale every freed warren drums the old warning-thump together as one heartbeat, while Bramble tells the Undersleeper the story of the journey, so it dreams a kind dream and settles back to sleep.
- **Battles never kill:** enemies under the Hum are "woken" or "shaken off" and wander home; creatures of the depths "fade back into the dark". Allies who fall are "worn out" and recover after the battle.
- Hero, friends, warrens, the elder, items and dialog: invented (see the design).
